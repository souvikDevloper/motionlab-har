How well does the signal hold up?

The same held-out windows face the same seeded perturbations for every deep model. Each condition is evaluated separately.

Cards show augmented CNN-LSTM results and the difference from the unaugmented CNN-LSTM.

What the perturbations mean

Gaussian noise: independent noise with standard deviation 0.1, 0.3 or 0.5 times the clean training channel standard deviation.

Orientation: one random rigid 3D rotation per window, shared by accelerometer and gyroscope, with angle sampled within ±15° or ±30°.

Missing block: 13 consecutive readings (10.16%) filled with clean training channel means.

Training augmentation: half the fit windows receive a rotation within ±20° and noise between 0.02σ and 0.30σ; a quarter of those also receive a missing block. Validation stays clean.

These experiments perturb UCI's already preprocessed windows. They do not establish accuracy on a different phone, placement, irregular sampling or live sensor stream.

The protocol

The original UCI participant split is preserved. Five of the 21 official training participants form a validation set; the other 16 fit the models and scalers. Every participant belongs to exactly one set, so overlapping windows from a person stay together.

Classical baselines compare two predefined parameter settings each. Neural networks use clean validation macro F1 for checkpoint selection, learning-rate adjustment and early stopping. Every model is selected before test predictions are computed.

The clean and augmented CNN-LSTM share their architecture, initialization seed and optimizer settings. This is a single-seed comparison; repeated seeds and participant folds are needed for stronger conclusions.

Running on the web

The four trained deep models are exported to ONNX with their original normalization included. Predictions run locally in a browser worker using WebAssembly. Uploaded sensor values are not sent to a prediction service. Browser preview corruptions are illustrative; the aggregate benchmark curves use the saved full test experiment.

The online app supports recorded examples and CSV inference. The downloadable Python project contains the training workflow, classical estimators and original Streamlit dashboard. Live phone capture, calibrated confidence and mobile hardware benchmarks remain extensions.
